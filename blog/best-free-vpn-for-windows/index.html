How did we test free VPNs on Windows?

Most "best free VPN" articles never tell you how they tested, so here is exactly what we did and what we did not do.

The machine. A Windows 10 Pro PC (22H2) on a home fibre connection in Bengaluru, India, running the current public release of OllaVPN for Windows, version 0.1.0.972. We kept everything else on the PC exactly as it was. No browser extensions, no other VPN software, no firewall tweaks.

What we measured, every time, with the VPN off and then on:

  • Latency, the average of ten pings to 1.1.1.1.
  • Your visible IP address and country, as seen by a public IP lookup service.
  • Which DNS server answers your lookups, using two independent "who is asking" DNS tests. This is how you spot a DNS leak.

What we did not do. We did not install the competing VPNs on the same PC. Running several VPN apps on one machine leaves drivers and firewall rules behind that make every later result unreliable. If a provider changes its free plan tomorrow, the link next to each number is where to check.

Why our location matters. Our test PC sits in India, so connecting to London or Toronto means our traffic crosses an ocean first. That adds latency no VPN can remove. If you live in Europe or North America, your speeds to nearby VPN servers will usually be closer to the plan's limit than ours.

Proton VPN Free gives you unlimited data, no ads and a kill switch on one device, from a Swiss company with open-source apps. The trade-off is that Proton picks your server for you, and streaming and P2P are paid-only.

Proton VPN is the free VPN most review sites put first, and for good reason. Choose Proton Free if you want unlimited data from a long-established provider and do not mind where your server is.

How do free VPNs make money, and why should you care?

QUICK ANSWER

Running VPN servers costs real money, so every free VPN is paid for somehow: by paying customers, by a company that funds it as a product, or by you, through ads or your data. The last kind is the one to avoid.

Before you install any free VPN on your PC, ask one question: who pays for this? A VPN server costs money every hour it runs, and every gigabyte you send costs bandwidth. There are three honest answers and one dishonest one.

  • Paying customers fund free users. Proton, Windscribe, PrivadoVPN, hide.me and TunnelBear all work this way. The free plan is a showroom for the paid one, which is why free plans are deliberately limited.
  • A company funds the service as its product. OllaVPN is free with no paid tier and is backed by Ollasoftware. The limits (10 Mbps, one device, fair use) are what make that affordable.
  • Ads and data. Some free VPNs show ads, sell "anonymised" browsing data to analytics firms, or route other people's traffic through your connection. Those are the ones to avoid.

Red flags to check before you install a free VPN on Windows:

  1. No clear privacy policy, or one that says the provider may share "aggregated" usage data with partners.
  2. Your device becomes part of the network. Some free VPNs route other users' traffic through your PC. Read the terms for words like "peer", "resource sharing" or "IP sharing".
  3. No company behind it, no address and no named people.
  4. The installer comes from a download site rather than the provider's own website or the Microsoft Store.
  5. Unlimited everything with no explanation of how it is paid for.

Gizmodo's Windows guide lists Hola, Betternet, Urban VPN and Turbo VPN among the free VPNs to avoid for logging concerns. Do your own reading, but if a free VPN fails two or more of the checks above, keep looking.

What Windows-specific problems do free VPNs run into?

QUICK ANSWER

Most Windows VPN problems come from four places: the tunnel driver, a Windows 11 24H2 change that breaks some VPN apps, SmartScreen warnings on installers, and leftover network adapters. Each has a simple fix.

Very few VPN guides cover Windows itself, yet Windows is where most VPN trouble actually happens. These are the issues we see most, with the fixes.

Why does my VPN say "connected" but nothing loads on Windows 11?

Windows 11 version 24H2 changed how some networking works. On PCs where a Windows feature called Virtual Machine Platform is switched off, VPN apps that use the "userspace" method of building a tunnel can connect successfully yet carry no traffic: the app says connected, and no website loads. OllaVPN avoids this in normal mode by using the WireGuardNT driver on these Windows builds. The obfuscation mode still needs the userspace method, so OllaVPN warns you when it detects this combination, and its command-line tool can switch the feature on for you with ollavpnctl enable-vmp (a restart is needed). With any other VPN, turning on Virtual Machine Platform under Turn Windows features on or off is the usual fix.

What are Wintun and WireGuardNT, and do I need to care?

A VPN on Windows needs a virtual network adapter to send your traffic through. WireGuard-based VPNs use one of two drivers. Wintun works on every supported version of Windows. WireGuardNT runs inside Windows itself and is faster and more efficient on newer Windows 11 builds. A good app chooses for you. OllaVPN uses WireGuardNT on Windows 11 24H2 and newer and Wintun elsewhere, and our test PC (Windows 10) used Wintun. You should never have to install either driver by hand.

Why does Windows warn me when I run the VPN installer?

Windows SmartScreen warns about downloads it has not seen many times yet, even when they are legitimate. Before clicking Run anyway, check two things: that you downloaded the installer from the provider's own website, and that the file's Properties → Digital Signatures tab shows a valid signature. A free VPN installer from a third-party download site is a much bigger risk than any SmartScreen warning.

Why do I have extra network adapters after uninstalling a VPN?

Every VPN adds a virtual adapter, and some uninstallers leave them behind. A pile of old adapters can confuse Windows and slow down a new VPN. Open Settings → Network & internet → Advanced network settings, look for adapters named after VPNs you have removed, and uninstall them from Device Manager if their software is gone.

Why is my VPN slower on Wi-Fi than on a cable?

Wi-Fi adds delay and drops packets more often than a cable, and an encrypted tunnel makes both more noticeable. If speed matters, test the VPN once on a cable. If it is much faster, the problem is your Wi-Fi signal, not the VPN.

How do you install a free VPN on Windows 10 or 11 safely?

QUICK ANSWER

Download the installer only from the provider's own website, check its digital signature, install it, sign in, and run a DNS leak test before you trust it. The whole job takes about five minutes.

These steps work for any reputable VPN. We use OllaVPN as the example.

  1. Download from the official site only. For OllaVPN that is the download page. Our Windows installer is a full, self-contained file of about 200 MB that includes everything the app needs, so nothing else is fetched during installation.

How do you check that your VPN is really working on Windows?

QUICK ANSWER

Check three things with the VPN on: that websites see the VPN's IP address and country, that a DNS leak test shows only the VPN's DNS servers, and that a WebRTC test in your browser does not reveal your real IP.

Never assume a VPN is working because the button turned green. Three quick checks catch almost every real problem.

  1. IP address. Visit any "what is my IP" page, such as our IP checker. With the VPN on, it should show the VPN's country, not your own.
  2. DNS leak. Run a DNS leak test. Every server listed should belong to your VPN, not your internet provider. Our guide to DNS leak tests explains how to read the results.
  3. WebRTC. Browsers can reveal your real IP through WebRTC, the technology behind in-browser video calls. Run our WebRTC leak test with the VPN on.

If your VPN has a built-in check, as OllaVPN does, use it too. But an outside test is the only kind you should fully trust, because it sees what the rest of the internet sees.

Should you use a VPN browser extension or a Windows app?

QUICK ANSWER

Use the Windows app. A browser extension only protects traffic inside that one browser, while a VPN app protects every program on your PC, including Windows Update, Teams, Outlook, games and other browsers.

Many people searching for a free VPN for PC end up installing a Chrome or Edge extension because it takes ten seconds. It is worth understanding what you give up.

A VPN extension is usually a proxy. It sends the traffic of that one browser through another server. Everything else on your PC, including your email app, your messaging app, Windows Update, cloud backup, games and any second browser, still goes straight to the internet with your real IP address. Most extensions also leave your DNS lookups with your internet provider and do not protect against WebRTC leaks unless you configure them carefully.

A VPN app creates a virtual network adapter in Windows and routes all of your PC's traffic through an encrypted tunnel. That is the only way to get a kill switch that actually blocks traffic when the connection drops, and the only way to keep your DNS inside the tunnel for every program.

There is one good use for extensions: changing your location for a single browser tab while the rest of your PC stays on your normal connection. For privacy and safety on public Wi-Fi, use the app.

How do you choose the best VPN server location on Windows?

QUICK ANSWER

For speed, pick the closest location to you. For a different country's version of a website, pick that country. Our own tests show why: from India, London was faster than Toronto because it is closer.

Free VPNs that let you choose a location give you a decision to make every time you connect. Here is the rule of thumb we use, backed by our measurements above.

  • For everyday privacy, choose the nearest location. Every extra thousand kilometres adds delay. In our tests, the trip from India to London added about 150 ms, and Toronto added about 245 ms. A single download through London ran faster than through Toronto, even though both sit behind the same 10 Mbps limit.
  • For a website that differs by country, choose that country. Search results, prices and news sites often change by location. Pick the country whose version you want to see.
  • For video calls and gaming, distance matters more than speed. A call needs low delay far more than it needs bandwidth. Choose the closest location even if it is not in your own country.
  • If a location feels slow, try another one. Busy servers and congested internet routes change during the day. Switching takes a few seconds.

On a plan with a speed limit, the location changes how much of that limit you can actually use. On a plan without one, it changes everything.

Should you leave your VPN on all the time on Windows?

QUICK ANSWER

Yes, if your VPN has no data cap. Set it to start with Windows and connect automatically, so you are protected before any app connects. On a capped plan, switch it on only when you need it, or you will run out of data in days.

The biggest risk with any VPN is forgetting to turn it on. The moment your laptop joins café Wi-Fi, a dozen programs connect to the internet before you open a browser. If the VPN is not running yet, all of that traffic goes out unprotected.

On a VPN with no monthly cap, the safest setup is to let it start with Windows and connect on its own. OllaVPN has two switches for this in Settings: Launch when Windows starts and Connect automatically when OllaVPN starts. With both on and the kill switch always active, your PC is protected from the moment you sign in.

On a capped plan such as Windscribe or PrivadoVPN, leaving the VPN on all the time is not realistic. Background updates and cloud sync alone can use several gigabytes a month. Use those plans selectively: switch on for public Wi-Fi, banking and anything sensitive, and off for large downloads at home.

Is a free VPN enough, or should you pay for one on Windows?

QUICK ANSWER

A free VPN is enough for privacy on public Wi-Fi, keeping your browsing away from your internet provider, and everyday work. Pay only if you need several devices at full speed, specific streaming catalogues, or torrenting.

Most people who search for a free VPN for PC want three things: privacy from their internet provider, safety on public Wi-Fi, and an IP address in a different country now and then. A good free VPN does all three.

A free VPN is enough when you:

  • Browse, work, shop and bank on your own PC.
  • Use café, hotel or airport Wi-Fi.
  • Make video calls (10 Mbps comfortably handles HD calls).
  • Want your internet provider kept out of your DNS lookups.

A paid VPN from another provider is worth considering when you:

  • Need five or more devices protected at full speed at the same time.
  • Want a particular streaming service's catalogue from another country.
  • Download large files with BitTorrent, which OllaVPN does not allow.
  • Need dedicated IP addresses or port forwarding for work.

There is no shame in paying for a VPN if you need those things. Just be wary of "free" offers that are really refundable subscriptions: if a card is required, it is not a free VPN.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a completely free VPN for Windows?

Yes. OllaVPN Free, Proton VPN Free, Windscribe Free, PrivadoVPN Free, hide.me Free and TunnelBear Free are all free forever without a card. OllaVPN has no paid tier at all; the others are free versions of paid services.

Which free VPN for Windows has no data limit?

OllaVPN Free and Proton VPN Free have no monthly data cap. OllaVPN applies a daily fair-use rule (full speed for the first 20 GB a day). hide.me has reportedly removed its cap; check its site.

Does Windows have a built-in free VPN?

Windows includes a VPN client that can connect to a VPN server you already have, often at work. It does not include a VPN service, so on its own it will not hide your traffic from your internet provider.

Is a free VPN safe to use on Windows?

A free VPN from a provider that explains how it pays its bills, publishes a clear privacy policy and offers its installer on its own website is safe to use. Free VPNs that show ads, share "aggregated" data or route other users' traffic through your PC are not.

How fast is a free VPN on Windows?

It depends on the plan's limit and your distance from the server. OllaVPN Free is limited to 10 Mbps, and on our test PC we got 8.6–8.8 Mbps through London. Capped plans such as PrivadoVPN can be faster until their monthly data runs out.

Can I use a free VPN for torrenting on Windows?

Some free plans allow it (PrivadoVPN Free, according to Gizmodo); OllaVPN, Proton and hide.me do not on their free plans. Always respect copyright law where you live.

Does a free VPN slow down my PC?

What you notice is network speed, which depends on the plan's limit and the server's distance, not the app. Encryption adds a small amount of work for the processor, which any PC from the last decade handles easily. A VPN can make some connections faster if your internet provider slows certain kinds of traffic.

Will a free VPN work on Windows 10 and Windows 11?

All six VPNs in this guide support both. On Windows 11 24H2, see the section above on "connected but nothing loads" if your VPN connects but no websites open.

Do I need an account to use a free VPN?

Most do. OllaVPN needs a verified email address; Windscribe works without an email but cuts your cap to 2 GB; hide.me asks for no email.
