1. What exactly does a free VPN protect on public Wi-Fi?

Quick Answer A VPN encrypts your traffic between your device and the VPN server, preventing anyone on the same public Wi-Fi from reading your browsing activity, passwords, or communications.

When you join an open Wi-Fi network at an airport, hotel, or cafe, your network packets travel over shared radio frequencies. Without a VPN, an attacker on the same network can intercept unencrypted packets and log metadata. A VPN wraps your traffic in an unbreakable WireGuard tunnel, ensuring that snoops only see scrambled ciphertext.

2. Why is public Wi-Fi actually risky, technically speaking?

Quick Answer Public Wi-Fi networks typically lack pre-shared encryption keys (open networks), allowing packet sniffing and local network spoofing by any connected client.

On home networks, WPA3 encryption isolates client traffic. On open public Wi-Fi, no local network-layer encryption exists. Every device broadcasts data across the same shared airwaves, enabling simple packet capture tools like Wireshark to inspect network headers, unencrypted DNS lookups, and unencrypted local traffic.

3. How does a man-in-the-middle attack work on open networks?

Quick Answer An attacker uses ARP spoofing or rogue DHCP servers to route your internet traffic through their machine, intercepting or altering data in real time.

In a Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attack, the attacker tricks your laptop or phone into thinking their computer is the default network gateway. When you attempt to reach websites, your requests pass through the attacker's device first. With a VPN active, your device establishes a secure cryptographic tunnel directly to the VPN exit node, rendering intermediate routing transparent and secure.

4. What is an "evil twin" hotspot, and how does a VPN help?

Quick Answer An Evil Twin is a rogue Wi-Fi access point broadcasted by an attacker with the exact same SSID as a legitimate cafe or hotel network.

If you connect to a fraudulent access point named "Starbucks_Guest_WiFi", the attacker controls the router. Even if they inspect all traffic passing through the access point, your VPN tunnel encrypts every byte with WireGuard and post-quantum keys. The attacker learns nothing except that an encrypted connection is active.

5. How does encryption keep your data unreadable on a shared network?

Quick Answer ChaCha20-Poly1305 and AES-256 ciphers convert your raw web requests into randomized mathematical ciphertext that cannot be decrypted without the private key.

Modern VPN tunnels establish session keys through a Diffie-Hellman handshake. Every outbound packet is encrypted at the network driver level before touching the Wi-Fi card, ensuring complete confidentiality from the device all the way to the VPN gateway.

6. Does a VPN protect you from every public Wi-Fi risk?

Quick Answer No. A VPN protects network transport. It does not prevent phishing scams, malware downloads, or physical shoulder-surfing.

As explained in our guide on whether a VPN can stop hackers, network encryption protects the transport layer. You must still practice basic hygiene: avoid suspicious downloads, verify website URLs, and use a password manager.

7. Why does post-quantum encryption matter for travelers specifically?

Quick Answer Travelers transmit work credentials, banking sessions, and passports over public networks that adversaries intercept today to decrypt later with quantum computing.

Under the "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" threat model, adversaries record encrypted traffic today. Post-quantum VPNs incorporate ML-KEM / Kyber algorithms to ensure that harvested data remains secure against future quantum computers.

Any network where you do not personally control the router and where untrusted strangers share the subnet warrants an active VPN connection.
