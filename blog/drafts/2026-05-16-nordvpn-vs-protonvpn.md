# NordVPN vs ProtonVPN in 2026 — a friendly, honest comparison

There are two names that come up in almost every "which VPN should I get?" conversation: **NordVPN** and **ProtonVPN**. Both have been around for years. Both have earned real reputations. And both are genuinely good at what they do — which is exactly why choosing between them isn't obvious.

This is a plain-English comparison written for people who don't want to wade through spec sheets. We'll cover what each one is *like to use*, what you actually pay, and which kinds of users tend to be happier with which. Toward the end, we'll also flag a newer option called **OllaVPN** that's worth a look if post-quantum cryptography is on your radar — more on that below.

No marketing language, no rankings, no winners and losers. Just an honest read.

---

## The short answer

If you want the quick version:

- **NordVPN** is the polished, all-in-one option. It's built for people who want a VPN plus a few extra security tools — a password manager, dark-web monitoring, ad-blocking — bundled into one app and one bill. It's the easier "set it and forget it" choice.
- **ProtonVPN** is the privacy-purist option. It's run by the same Swiss team behind Proton Mail, and the whole product is shaped by that philosophy: minimal data collection, open-source apps, and a genuinely usable free tier. It's the choice when *who is running the VPN* matters as much as the VPN itself.

You can't really go wrong with either. The choice is more about which philosophy you prefer.

## What you actually pay

Pricing for both services changes often, especially around sales, but here's a snapshot of what each company is advertising today.

### NordVPN

NordVPN sells four tiers — Basic, Plus, Complete, and Prime — and each one adds more security extras on top of the VPN itself. The 2-year plan is where most of the value sits.

On the 2-year plan:

- **Basic** — about **$3.09/month**. Just the VPN.
- **Plus** — about **$3.59/month**. VPN plus a password manager and malware-protection feature.
- **Complete** — about **$4.99/month**. Adds encrypted cloud storage and a wider security suite.
- **Prime** — about **$6.99/month**. Adds identity-theft tools.

A 1-month plan starts around **$12.99/month** and a 1-year plan around **$4.99/month** for Basic. All tiers cover up to **10 devices** and come with a **30-day money-back guarantee**.

### ProtonVPN

ProtonVPN keeps it simpler with three plans:

- **Proton Free** — **$0**. One device at a time, choice of servers in 10 countries (the app picks one for you). No data cap and no ads.
- **VPN Plus** — about **$2.99/month** on the 2-year plan. 10 devices, full server network across 140+ countries, streaming and torrenting support, NetShield ad/malware blocker.
- **Proton Unlimited** — about **$7.99/month** on the 2-year plan. Bundles VPN Plus with Proton Mail Plus, Proton Drive Plus, Proton Pass, and a calendar — essentially the full Proton suite.

Also a **30-day money-back guarantee** on paid plans.

### How they compare on price

If you only want a VPN, **ProtonVPN Plus is meaningfully cheaper than NordVPN Basic** at the 2-year tier. If you want a VPN *plus* a whole ecosystem of privacy apps, **Proton Unlimited and NordVPN Complete land in a similar price range**, but they bundle different things (Proton gives you email and storage; Nord gives you a password manager, cloud backup, and identity tools).

## What they're like to use

This is the part the spec sheets miss.

### NordVPN

NordVPN feels like a product made by a big consumer-software company. The app is bright, friendly, and the world map you tap to connect is genuinely fun. Connections happen fast. The mobile and desktop apps are consistent. If a family member who doesn't think of themselves as "techie" needs a VPN, this is the one most people would point them to without a second thought.

Where the experience shines is in the bundled features. The password manager and dark-web monitor in higher tiers feel like real products, not afterthought add-ons. NordVPN is based in **Panama**, has been audited multiple times by independent firms, and uses its own implementation of **WireGuard** (called NordLynx) under the hood.

### ProtonVPN

ProtonVPN feels different. The app is calmer, more text-and-tables, and quietly serious. The free tier is the standout: it has **no data cap**, no ads, no time limit, and no upsell nags during a session. That alone makes it one of the friendliest free VPNs on the market.

Paid users get features the privacy-aware crowd cares about — **Secure Core** routes traffic through a hardened server in a privacy-friendly country before sending it onward, and **NetShield** blocks ads and trackers at the DNS layer. Everything Proton ships is **open source**, and the company is based in **Switzerland**, which has some of the strongest data-protection laws in Europe.

### Which one is more "fun"?

NordVPN, by a small margin. The map, the polish, the integrated extras — it has the smoother on-ramp.

### Which one is more reassuring?

ProtonVPN. The open-source codebase, Swiss jurisdiction, and the parent company's privacy track record are hard to argue with for users who think carefully about who they trust.

## Speed, streaming, and the everyday stuff

Both VPNs use **WireGuard** (or a WireGuard variant), which is the modern protocol behind almost every fast VPN you can buy today. In practice that means both will feel fast on a decent home internet connection.

Streaming works on both, with the usual caveats — every streaming service plays cat-and-mouse with every VPN, so individual servers may or may not unlock individual services on a given day. Both companies invest real effort in keeping the access working.

For torrenting, both support P2P traffic on the appropriate servers. ProtonVPN's free tier doesn't allow P2P; you'd need VPN Plus or higher.

For gaming or video calls, both are perfectly usable, especially if you pick a nearby server.

## Privacy posture

This is where the two companies' values are most visible.

**ProtonVPN** is open source across all its apps, publishes regular transparency reports, and is owned by Proton AG — a company whose whole brand is built on privacy. Switzerland's legal framework also limits the data Proton can be compelled to hand over.

**NordVPN** runs a closed-source app but has been independently audited multiple times by firms like Deloitte and PwC. It operates from Panama, which has no mandatory data-retention laws for VPN providers, and its no-logs policy has been re-audited regularly. After a 2018 server-config incident — which the company disclosed publicly and addressed — it overhauled its infrastructure, including a shift to diskless RAM-only servers.

Both companies are responsive when issues surface. They just communicate differently — Proton with the tone of a privacy-rights nonprofit, Nord with the tone of a consumer-software brand.

## So which one should you pick?

Here's a simple way to think about it.

### NordVPN is probably right for you if:
- You want **one app and one bill** to cover several things: VPN, password manager, malware blocking, identity monitoring.
- You like a polished, **map-driven interface** and want the easiest "click and you're protected" experience.
- You watch a lot of streaming content from different regions and want a VPN that consistently invests in unblocking access.
- You're shopping for a **family** and want gift-able, simple plans across up to 10 devices.

### ProtonVPN is probably right for you if:
- You care about **who runs the service** as much as the service itself — open source, Swiss-based, transparent.
- You want a genuinely usable **free tier** without time limits or data caps.
- You already use (or want to use) **Proton Mail, Drive, or Pass**, and the Unlimited bundle makes sense for your life.
- You like the idea of **Secure Core** routing for extra-sensitive sessions.
