8. Why should you care about post-quantum encryption for your VPN?

You should care about post-quantum encryption for your VPN because it protects your long-term data security against future attacks from quantum computers.

Most encryption used today, including what’s in standard VPNs, is vulnerable to attacks from powerful quantum computers. While these machines aren’t quite here yet, they’re on the horizon. This means that data encrypted today could potentially be decrypted years from now by someone who records it and waits for quantum computing to catch up. For a VPN, which is all about privacy and keeping your online activity secure, that’s a pretty big problem if you’re thinking about long-term data security.

Here at OllaVPN, we’re not waiting for that future to arrive. We’ve taken steps to ensure your connection is protected against this emerging threat with what we call future-proof encryption. We do this by implementing a unique hybrid handshake. This means that when you connect to OllaVPN, your session keys are established using a combination of traditional cryptography and quantum-resistant algorithms. Even if one method is eventually broken by a quantum computer, the other remains secure, keeping your data private.

Think of it as building a bridge with two different types of steel. If one type of steel eventually rusts away, the other still holds the bridge up. This approach makes OllaVPN a post-quantum-ready alternative that aims to keep your browsing history and personal information safe not just today, but decades from now. It’s about protecting your privacy against threats that don’t even fully exist yet, ensuring your digital footprint stays yours.

14. A vetting checklist for any VPN comparison you read

Not every “X vs Y” article on the internet uses a defensible methodology. When you read someone else’s comparison, watch for these patterns:

Affiliate-driven rankings. Many comparison sites are funded by VPN affiliate commissions. Operators that pay higher commissions tend to win those comparisons. The tell is rankings that don’t match the actual technical merits or audit history - and “best for streaming” being the default lead category, because streaming-VPN affiliate payouts are highest.

Stale data. VPN operators change pricing, ownership, jurisdiction, and feature sets continuously. A comparison written in 2023 and republished in 2026 without re-checking is unreliable. Look for a “last updated” date and a methodology section before trusting the comparison.

Missing audit attribution. Trustworthy comparisons cite the audit firm and year for each operator’s last independent audit. If a “no-logs” claim is repeated without saying who verified it and when, the claim is doing less work than it appears to.

No coverage of what each operator does NOT do. Honest comparisons include the limitations and non-features of each operator. A comparison that only lists positives is selling, not informing.
