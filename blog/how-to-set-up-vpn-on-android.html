What are the alternatives to using a VPN app for Android setup?

QUICK ANSWER

You can configure VPNs manually on Android using built-in settings or third-party client apps, but it's often more complex and less feature-rich than using a dedicated app.

You can configure VPNs manually on Android using built-in settings or third-party client apps, but it's often more complex and less feature-rich than using a dedicated app.

While using the OllaVPN app is the easiest and most recommended way to secure your Android device, you do have alternatives, though they come with trade-offs. Android has built-in support for various VPN protocols, allowing for a **manual setup**. You can usually find these options deep in your network settings. Historically, this included protocols like PPTP (which is now completely deprecated due to security flaws) and L2TP/IPsec. While L2TP/IPsec offers better security than PPTP, it's still generally less efficient and performant than modern protocols.

Beyond the built-in options, you could also use a generic **OpenVPN client** app. This would require you to download configuration files from your VPN provider and import them into the OpenVPN app. While this gives you access to a more secure and robust protocol than the older built-in options, it still lacks many of the quality-of-life features you get with a dedicated VPN application. Things like an automatic kill switch, easy server switching, or even just a simple on/off toggle are often missing or require additional manual configuration.

Ultimately, while these manual methods offer some flexibility, they often introduce more **complexity** and a **lack of features** compared to a purpose-built VPN app like OllaVPN. You'll spend more time configuring and troubleshooting, and you might miss out on essential privacy and security features that protect you automatically.

What other steps can I take to keep my Android secure?

QUICK ANSWER

Beyond a VPN, you can secure your Android by managing app permissions, keeping software updated, using strong passwords and two-factor authentication, and being careful on public Wi-Fi.

Beyond a VPN, you can secure your Android by managing app permissions, keeping software updated, using strong passwords and two-factor authentication, and being careful on public Wi-Fi.

A VPN is a crucial layer of defense for your Android, but it's not the only one. Think of it as part of a larger security toolkit. One of the biggest things you can do is pay attention to your **app permissions**. Many apps ask for more access than they actually need to function. A how-to guide is only useful if you can verify the outcome. After completing the steps above, the easiest checks to confirm the configuration is in place:

**Visible state in the app.** The OllaVPN menu-bar or system-tray icon should be green (connected) and any status panel should show the country you selected. If the icon is gray or amber, the tunnel didn't fully come up, retry the connect.

**IP address changed.** Open our what-is-my-IP tool. The IP shown should be the exit's IP, not your real ISP's IP, and the country shown should match the country you selected in the app. If you still see your real IP, the tunnel isn't carrying traffic, the most common cause is a firewall on the local network blocking WireGuard's UDP/51820. **DNS is going through the tunnel.** Open our DNS lookup tool. The resolver IP shown should be the in-tunnel one (CGNAT 100.64.x.x range), not your ISP's DNS. If you see your ISP's resolver, DNS is leaking, see our in-tunnel DNS page for the technical details on why this happens and how OllaVPN prevents it.

**No WebRTC leak.** Open our WebRTC leak test. You should see only the exit's IPv4 address. If your real IP appears (private LAN address or public IPv4/IPv6), WebRTC is bypassing the VPN, either OllaVPN's leak prevention has failed (file a report at `[[email protected]](/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection)`) or your browser has a WebRTC-specific privacy issue we can help diagnose.

If a step fails, here’s how to debug

QUICK ANSWER

The most common failure modes for any VPN setup task on Windows, macOS, or Android, in order of frequency:

The most common failure modes for any VPN setup task on Windows, macOS, or Android, in order of frequency:

**The VPN refuses to connect at all.** Almost always a network-side issue: a restrictive firewall on the local network (school, corporate, hotel) blocking WireGuard's default UDP/51820 port. Try a different network briefly to confirm it's network-side. On Frankfurt the obfuscated MASQUE-on-port-443 transport works around many restrictive firewalls, switch to it in the country picker.

**The VPN connects but the internet is broken.** Almost always a DNS issue. The in-tunnel resolver hasn't taken precedence (sometimes after a macOS update or a Windows feature update, the supplemental DNS keys get cleared). Restarting the VPN typically fixes it; full disconnect and reconnect re-installs the DNS configuration.

**Some apps work but others don't.** Either the app has its own proxy settings configured separately (some corporate apps), or the app uses a protocol the VPN doesn't carry (very rare, almost everything is TCP/UDP and works fine through the tunnel). Check the app's settings before assuming the VPN is at fault.

**Speed is much slower than expected.** The exit you picked is either congested or geographically far from you. Try a closer exit (the country picker shows latency). The free-tier 10 Mbps cap is the ceiling regardless of exit choice; if you need more, the Pro tier at $2/mo annual lifts it to 10 Gbps.
