Which VPN Offers Better Speed and Uses Which Protocols?

It's hard to crown a single winner on speed because it depends heavily on your connection, location, and the specific server you're connecting to, but generally, WireGuard-based VPNs like OllaVPN offer superior performance.

When you're talking about VPN speed, you're really looking at a few factors: raw throughput (how many megabits per second you can push), latency (how long it takes for data to go back and forth), and consistency. Many VPNs use **OpenVPN**, which is a very robust and secure protocol, but it can be a bit heavy and often slower than newer options. Then there's **WireGuard**, which is designed to be much lighter, faster, and more efficient. It uses simpler cryptography and has a significantly smaller codebase, making it easier to audit and generally quicker. Some providers also roll out their own **custom protocols**. These are often based on existing open-source protocols but heavily modified, sometimes to improve speed, sometimes to bypass stricter network restrictions, or sometimes to differentiate themselves. The challenge with custom protocols is that they aren't always as transparent or as widely audited as open-source alternatives like WireGuard, so it's harder to verify their security claims. When we look at speed tests, WireGuard consistently comes out ahead in terms of both throughput and lower latency, which is why we built OllaVPN around it. At OllaVPN, we give you 10 Mbps on our free plan, which is more than enough for streaming HD video, video calls, and most online gaming, and that's running on WireGuard. If you need more, OllaVPN Plus bumps that up to 10 Gbps on up to five devices for just $2 a month. The protocol choice makes a huge difference here. Because WireGuard is so efficient, we can offer those speeds without bogging down our servers, ensuring that your connection stays fast and responsive, whether you're on the free tier or Plus.

This isn't just a hypothetical threat; governments and major corporations are investing heavily in quantum computing research. If your communications today are intercepted and stored, a quantum computer in five or ten years could potentially decrypt them, exposing your past private activities. That's where \*\*future-proof encryption\*\* comes in. It's about designing cryptographic systems that can withstand attacks from these quantum computers. Most VPNs today use algorithms that are strong against classical computers but vulnerable to quantum ones. We're talking about protecting your data not just for today, but for decades to come. Think about it: if someone records your encrypted traffic now, they might not be able to read it. But with a quantum computer, that could change. OllaVPN tackles this head-on by implementing a \*\*hybrid handshake\*\*. This means when you connect to our VPN, we use a combination of a classical, proven encryption algorithm \*and\* a post-quantum algorithm. So, even if one of them is broken (either by a quantum computer or some other unforeseen vulnerability), your connection remains secure because the other algorithm is still protecting your data. This dual-layer approach provides a much stronger, more resilient defense against future threats. This commitment to \*\*post-quantum-ready\*\* encryption is a key OllaVPN differentiator. While many VPNs are still waiting for quantum computers to become a more immediate threat, we believe in being proactive. A vetting checklist for any VPN comparison you read

QUICK ANSWER

Not every "X vs Y" article on the internet uses a defensible methodology. When you read someone else's comparison, watch for these patterns:

Not every "X vs Y" article on the internet uses a defensible methodology. When you read someone else's comparison, watch for these patterns:

**Affiliate-driven rankings.** Many comparison sites are funded by VPN affiliate commissions. Operators that pay higher commissions tend to win those comparisons. The tell is rankings that don't match the actual technical merits or audit history, and "best for streaming" being the default lead category, because streaming-VPN affiliate payouts are highest.

**Stale data.** VPN operators change pricing, ownership, jurisdiction, and feature sets continuously. A comparison written in 2023 and republished in 2026 without re-checking is unreliable. Look for a "last updated" date and a methodology section before trusting the comparison.

**Missing audit attribution.** Trustworthy comparisons cite the audit firm and year for each operator's last independent audit. If a "no-logs" claim is repeated without saying who verified it and when, the claim is doing less work than it appears to.

**No coverage of what each operator does NOT do.** Honest comparisons include the limitations and non-features of each operator. A comparison that only lists positives is selling, not informing.
